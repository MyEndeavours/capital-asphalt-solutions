A proven material

A Brief History of Mastic Asphalt

Mastic asphalt has been used as a construction and waterproofing material for centuries.

Its origins can be traced back to naturally occurring asphalt deposits, which were used by ancient civilisations for waterproofing and construction. The material developed considerably with the discovery and commercial use of natural asphalt and, later, refined bitumen.

Mastic asphalt became particularly important in the UK during the 19th and 20th centuries. Its waterproofing properties made it valuable for buildings, bridges, roads, footways, basements and other structures where protection from water was critical.

The material has continued to evolve alongside modern construction. Today's mastic asphalt is manufactured to controlled specifications, with carefully selected aggregates and bituminous binders designed to provide consistent performance.

Despite the development of many modern waterproofing products, mastic asphalt remains used because of its combination of water resistance, durability, versatility and long service life.

What is it?

A Dense, Virtually Impermeable Material

Mastic asphalt is a dense material consisting primarily of bitumen and mineral aggregate.

When heated to the appropriate temperature, the material becomes workable and can be applied to prepared surfaces. Once laid and cooled, it forms a dense, continuous layer.

One of its key characteristics is its ability to create a seamless waterproofing surface. Correctly installed mastic asphalt has no conventional joints or seams across the main waterproofed area, reducing potential routes for water penetration.

Depending on the application, it can be used as a waterproofing layer, protective surface or wearing surface, and can form part of a wider roofing or flooring system.

UK construction

How Mastic Asphalt Is Used in the UK

Mastic asphalt is used in a wide variety of applications across the UK construction industry.

⌂

Roofs

Mastic asphalt is widely used for flat and low-pitched roofs where reliable waterproofing is required. It can be installed over suitable roof decks and substrates to create a continuous waterproof layer.

▱

Balconies & Terraces

Balconies are exposed to rain, temperature changes and regular foot traffic. Mastic asphalt can provide a robust waterproof layer beneath the finished surface, helping protect the underlying structure from water ingress.

↗

Stairs & Steps

External stairs and steps can present difficult waterproofing challenges because of their shape, junctions and exposure to rain. Mastic asphalt can be applied to suitable stair structures to create a continuous waterproofing layer.

＋

Walkways & Other Areas

Mastic asphalt is also used for walkways, podium decks, access areas, basements, tanking applications and a variety of other construction projects. The specification depends on the structure and intended use.

Preparation, Detailing & Workmanship Matter

Mastic asphalt is not simply a material that is placed over a surface to stop water getting through.

A successful installation depends on the condition of the substrate, preparation, detailing, material specification and workmanship.

Particular attention needs to be paid to roof edges, perimeter details, drainage outlets, upstands and abutments, stair edges, balcony thresholds and changes between different materials.
